- **Where does the FCF the company generates go?**

**Prompt:**

> Analyze all the documents and answer as clearly as possible: What priority does the company give to the following? Reinvestment in the business, share buybacks, debt repayment, dividend payments and cash accumulation.

![Alphabet NotebookLM](/images/M4/M2/D2/ES-M4-M2-D2-Alphabet-NotebookLM.png)

{{% details title="Click to read the full answer" closed="true" %}}

Based on 2025 financial reports and management statements, Alphabet sets a clear order of priorities for the use of its capital, placing **technology growth and AI infrastructure above any other objective**.

The priority hierarchy according to the documents is set out below:

### 1. Reinvestment in the business (Top priority)
The company identifies reinvestment as its **primary use of capital** to ensure long-term growth.
*   **Investment in AI and infrastructure:** In 2025, Alphabet allocated **$91.447 billion** to capital expenditure (CapEx), focused on servers and data centers.
*   **Massive acceleration:** For 2026, the priority increases sharply, with projected CapEx of **$175 to $185 billion** to meet demand for AI computing.
*   **R&D:** Investment in research and development totaled **$61.087 billion** in 2025, a 24% year-on-year increase to strengthen its Gemini models.

### 2. Share buybacks
This is the **main mechanism for returning capital to shareholders**, receiving a much larger allocation of funds than dividends.
*   **Volume:** During 2025, the company allocated **$45.398 billion** to repurchase and retire 240 million of its own shares.
*   **Future commitment:** The board of directors authorized a new **$70 billion** program in April 2025, of which $69.5 billion remains available.

### 3. Cash accumulation and acquisitions
Alphabet maintains an extremely strong liquidity position to fund "strategic opportunities" and strengthen the balance sheet.
*   **Reserve:** As of year-end 2025, the company had **$126.843 billion** in cash and marketable securities.
*   **Strategic acquisitions:** This cash is vital for large-scale acquisitions already committed for 2026, such as **Wiz ($32 billion)** and **Intersect ($4.8 billion)**.

### 4. Dividend payments
Although an established priority, it is **secondary to share buybacks**.
*   **Figures:** In 2025, Alphabet paid **$10.049 billion** in total dividends, less than a quarter of what it allocated to buybacks.
*   **Policy:** The company pays **$0.21 per share** quarterly but emphasizes that these payments are discretionary and evaluated according to capital structure and market conditions.

### 5. Debt repayment (Low priority)
Debt reduction is **not a current strategic priority**. In fact, Alphabet is increasing its leverage to fund its operations and capital needs.
*   **Increase in debt:** In 2025, the company issued new debt with a net value of **$37.3 billion**.
*   **Trend:** Long-term debt rose from $10.883 billion in 2024 to **$46.547 billion in 2025**. Although it made payments of $32.427 billion, the overall strategy has been to raise new funding to support its ambitious expansion plans.

- **Dividend payments: has the company ever announced a flexible dividend or scrip dividend in its history?**

**Prompt:**

> Analyze all the documents and answer as clearly as possible: Has the company ever declared a flexible dividend in its history?

![Alphabet NotebookLM Part 2](/images/M4/M2/D2/ES-M4-M2-D2-Alphabet-NotebookLM-parte2.png)

{{% details title="Click to read the full answer" closed="true" %}}

Based on Alphabet's financial documents for 2023, 2024 and 2025, **the company has not declared a "flexible dividend"** (also known as *scrip dividend*, where the shareholder chooses between cash or new shares).

The nature and recent history of its dividends according to the source are set out below:

### 1. Nature of dividends
Alphabet maintains a **cash-only dividend program**. The company states that its program pays "regular cash dividends" to holders of Class A, Class B and Class C shares. Nowhere in the reports is there any mention of the option to receive the dividend in shares or under a flexible arrangement.

### 2. History of the dividend program
Over the periods reported in the documents, the following trend is observed:
*   **2023:** Alphabet **did not pay dividends** to its shareholders during this period.
*   **2024:** The company began paying dividends, declaring a total of **$0.60 per share** for the year.
*   **2025:** Alphabet increased its payout, declaring a total of **$0.83 per share**. In April 2025, the Board of Directors increased the quarterly dividend by 5%, setting it at **$0.21 per share**.

### 3. Future commitments
The company has stated its intention to continue paying **quarterly cash dividends** in the future, although it clarifies that any future decision is at the discretion of the Board of Directors based on factors such as earnings, liquidity and market conditions.

**In conclusion**, Alphabet's dividend history is recent (starting in 2024) and has been strictly limited to **cash** payments, alongside an aggressive **share buyback** program as its main method of returning capital to shareholders.
