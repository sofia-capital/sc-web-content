### Meeting targets

We look for the team to be conservative in their estimates; if they miss, the market will punish them.

**Prompt:**

> Analyze all the documents and answer as clearly as possible: What targets has the management team set? Have they met them? If not, specify the reasons if possible.

{{% details title="Click to read the full answer" closed="true" %}}

Alphabet's management team, led by CEO Sundar Pichai, set a series of strategic goals for fiscal 2025 focused on consolidating its position as an **"AI-first"** company and accelerating growth in its business pillars.

The goals set and the degree to which they were met, according to the documents, are set out below:

### 1. Goals met (strategic successes)

*   **AI leadership and adoption:** The goal of launching cutting-edge AI models was met with the launch of **Gemini 3**, which has had the fastest adoption of any model in the company's history, processing 3 times more tokens per day than the previous version.
*   **Global revenue milestone:** Management achieved annual revenue exceeding **$400 billion** for the first time in the company's history.
*   **Google Cloud acceleration:** The goal was to make the cloud an AI engine for businesses. They achieved **48%** revenue growth in the quarter, reaching an annual run rate of over **$70 billion** and a record backlog of **$240 billion**.
*   **Search ecosystem expansion:** The goal of transforming search through AI was met; Google Search saw **more usage in Q4 2025 than ever before**, with 17% revenue growth.
*   **Operational efficiency:** They reduced Gemini service unit costs by **78%** during 2025, a critical goal for scaling AI profitably.
*   **YouTube and subscriptions growth:** YouTube exceeded **$60 billion** in annual revenue and the company reached more than **325 million paid subscriptions** in total.
*   **Waymo scalability:** They met the goal of expanding commercial autonomous driving, exceeding **20 million trips** and completing more than 400,000 weekly journeys.

### 2. Goals not met or pending challenges

Despite overall success, there are areas where targets were not reached or setbacks were faced:

*   **Google Network performance:** Revenue in this segment **fell 2%** in the fourth quarter.
    *   **Reason:** Mainly due to a decline in **AdSense** revenue.
*   **Fully meeting AI demand:** The CEO admitted that the company is operating in a **"supply-constrained"** manner.
    *   **Reason:** Constraints in the AI chip supply chain, as well as shortages of **power, water and land** to expand data centers at the speed the market demands.
*   **Regulatory stability:** Management did not succeed in avoiding adverse court rulings on antitrust.
    *   **Reason:** Unfavorable verdicts in cases such as the **DOJ (Search)** and **Epic Games (Play Store)**, which require Alphabet to implement "structural remedies" and major changes to its software distribution models.
*   **Profitability in "Other Bets" and hardware:**
    *   **Other Bets:** Still not profitable, with an annual operating loss of **$7.515 billion**.
    *   **Hardware:** Pixel device margins continue to have an **"adverse effect"** on the company's consolidated margins due to price pressure and high selling costs.

**In summary:** The management team has successfully met its **technology innovation and revenue growth** goals in the **core business (Search and Cloud)**, but faces difficulties in stabilizing its external ad network, meeting physical infrastructure demand and addressing legal and regulatory pressures that threaten its distribution model.

### Salaries, stock-based pay and incentives

**Prompt:**

> Analyze all the documents and answer as clearly as possible: What is the management team's salary? What are their incentives?

{{% details title="Click to read the full answer" closed="true" %}}
According to 2024 and 2025 financial and corporate governance reports, Alphabet's executive compensation is designed around a **"pay for performance"** philosophy, where most compensation is tied to long-term share value.

Salaries and the incentive system are set out below:

### 1. Base salaries (annual)
Base salary represents a smaller portion of executives' total compensation, providing stable income in line with their contributions:
*   **Sundar Pichai (CEO):** **$2,000,000**.
*   **Anat Ashkenazi (CFO), Ruth Porat (President/CIO), Philipp Schindler (SVP/CBO) and Kent Walker (President of Global Affairs):** **$1,000,000** each.
*   **Larry Page and Sergey Brin (Co-founders):** Receive a symbolic salary of **$1** per year.

### 2. Incentives and variable compensation
Alphabet's incentive system is split into short- and long-term components:

*   **Stock-Based Compensation (SBC – main incentive):** This is the most important component, designed to align management and shareholder interests. It is granted through:
    *   **Google Stock Units (GSUs):** Class C share units that **vest** over time (typically over 4 years), encouraging retention.
    *   **Performance Stock Units (PSUs):** Units whose final payout depends on company performance. The key factor is Alphabet's **Total Shareholder Return (TSR)** versus **S&P 100** companies. Payout can range from **0% to 200%** of the initial target value.
*   **Cash bonus (SVP bonus):** Historically, senior vice presidents (SVP) had a discretionary annual bonus of up to **$2,000,000** based on individual and company performance.
    *   **Strategic change:** This bonus was **discontinued in 2025**; management decided to move that $2 million target value directly into annual **PSU** grants to emphasize long-term performance.
*   **Dividend equivalents:** Executives with units that have not yet vested are entitled to payments equivalent to declared dividends, which are settled in Class C shares under the same vesting conditions as the original award.

### 3. Minimum stock ownership requirements
To ensure long-term commitment, Alphabet requires executives to maintain minimum personal investment levels in the company:
*   **CEO and founders:** Must own shares worth at least **$35,000,000**.
*   **Senior vice presidents (SVP):** Must own shares worth at least **$7,500,000**.

### 4. Other benefits and special incentives
*   **Security and transport:** Because of his public profile, Alphabet provides Sundar Pichai with **personal security** services (valued at over $8 million in 2024), as well as use of company cars and aircraft for certain executives for efficiency and security reasons.
*   **Signing bonuses:** In specific cases such as CFO Anat Ashkenazi, a **cash signing bonus of $9.9 million** and special stock grants were awarded to compensate for incentives lost when leaving her previous company.

**Summary:** The management team receives competitive but flat base salaries, while the vast majority of their potential wealth depends on **share price appreciation** and outperforming their **S&P 100** peers.

### How many shares in the company do they own?

**Prompt:**

> Analyze all the documents and answer as clearly as possible: Is it possible to know how many shares they own in the company? Can we know the percentage of the total?

{{% details title="Click to read the full answer" closed="true" %}}
Yes, it is entirely possible to know how many shares Alphabet's executives own and what percentage of the total that represents, thanks to the **Proxy Statement** and the 10-K annual report.

Share ownership of the main leaders and the management group as of **April 8, 2025** is set out below:

### 1. Main shareholders (founders and CEO)
The founders maintain control of the company through Class B shares (which have 10 votes per share), while the CEO mainly holds Class A shares (1 vote per share):

*   **Larry Page (Co-founder):** Owns **389,051,160 Class B shares**, representing **45.5%** of that class and **27.1% of total voting power**.
*   **Sergey Brin (Co-founder):** Owns **362,766,628 Class B shares**, equivalent to **42.4%** of that class and **25.2% of total voting power**.
*   **Sundar Pichai (CEO):** Owns **227,560 Class A shares**, representing less than 1% of the total of that class and of voting power. (It is worth noting that he was recently **awarded 1,343,392 additional shares** after meeting performance targets, which add to his net worth.)

### 2. Ownership by the management team and board (combined)
If we consider the **15 members** that make up senior management and the Board of Directors as a single group, their holdings are:
*   **Class A shares:** 3,108,903 shares (less than 1% of the class).
*   **Class B shares:** 774,166,728 shares, representing **90.5%** of all supervoting shares.
*   **Total voting power:** This group controls **53.9% of total votes** at Alphabet, ensuring that the founders and management retain strategic control.

### 3. Share class considerations
To understand these percentages, it is necessary to distinguish between the three types of shares the company issues:
*   **Class A (GOOGL):** One vote per share. There are approximately **5.822 billion** shares outstanding.
*   **Class B:** **Ten votes per share**, not traded on the stock exchange and held almost exclusively by the founders and executives. There are **837 million** shares.
*   **Class C (GOOG):** No voting rights. There are **5.438 billion** shares. These shares are typically used for employee compensation (SBC) but are generally not included in "voting ownership" tables in reports.

**In summary:** The exact figures can be known. The most relevant point is that although executives own a minority share of total economic ownership, they control more than **53% of voting power**, led by Larry Page and Sergey Brin, who together own almost **90% of Class B shares**.
